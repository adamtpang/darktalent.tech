# How to gauge "players" in tech and business

Written 2026-09-26. Companion to `MONEYBALL.md` and `TALENT_SCOUTS.md`.
Every metric here is a hypothesis until placements show which ones
actually predict hires.

## The rule

Measure what someone else relied on or paid for, because that is hard to
fake. Follower counts and likes are the batting average of tech: visible,
easy to game, and overpriced.

## Four tests for any metric

- **Costly to fake.** A paying customer beats a thousand likes.
- **Chosen by someone else.** Other people depended on it, merged it,
  bought it, or rehired the person.
- **Shows a trend.** A rising slope beats a high level (Cowen's stamina).
- **Tied to a role.** A great engineer and a great seller leave different
  traces.

## Where to look, by kind of player

| Player | Best public signal | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Engineer | Packages others depend on, merged PRs into other people's repos, shipped apps | GitHub, npm, PyPI, app stores |
| Founder / builder | Revenue, users, things shipped and still live | Stripe-verified pages, launches, live sites |
| Seller | Deals closed, quota hit, repeat buyers | Mostly private: references or proof |
| Marketer / creator | Replies and saves per follower, audience growth rate, conversions | X, Instagram, YouTube, newsletters |
| Writer / thinker | Cited and referenced by respected people | X, Substack, papers |
| Operator | Scale they ran, what improved under them | References, work samples |

## Overpriced (treat as a discount)

Follower counts, raw stars, school, big-tech logos, years of experience,
job titles.

## Underpriced (the on-base-percentage candidates)

- Code or products other people depend on
- Output that is accelerating year over year
- Owning one number end to end
- Small but deeply engaged audiences
- Unpaid, unwatched work: side projects, weekend builds

## Honest limits

- **GitHub only covers engineers.** darktalent measures that slice well.
- **X and Instagram measure attention, not ability.** Useful for creators
  and marketers, mostly noise for everyone else.
- **Sales and operations talent barely shows in public.** It needs a work
  sample or references.
- **None of this is proven.** Each metric is a guess until outcomes test it.

## Recommendation

Stay with engineers on GitHub until outcomes exist. Adding X or Instagram
scoring now widens the measurement before knowing whether any of it
predicts a hire. Get first placements, test the engine against them, then
expand one role at a time.
